Mornings · Legs×2 · Push · Pull · Back+Arms

Train like an athlete.

Two leg days for the hips and the knee, one push, one pull, and a back-and-arms combo day — the volume tilts toward your priorities, legs and back, while the Saturday game stands in as your weekend lower-body stress test.

A few principles.

  • Two leg days, staggered on purpose — Monday goes heavy and explosive while you're fresh; Thursday goes lighter and unilateral so it doesn't bleed into Saturday's game.
  • Back and hips lead the week — push the pull-ups, rows, and hip work hardest, and add a rep or a pound there before anywhere else.
  • Lead every single-leg and single-arm movement with the left, then match the right to it. That's how the gap closes instead of widening.
  • Friday's combo day is for back and arms — keep the pressing light there so Tuesday stays your real push day. It's also the session to drop first on a 4-day week.
  • Mornings = stiff body. The warm-up isn't optional, especially before pressing or pulling heavy.
  • Sleep and protein move the needle as much as the workout.
