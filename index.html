How the week is built Four hard lifts run Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday as an upper/lower split — posterior chain & hinge Monday, quad & single-leg stability Thursday — spaced so your left knee gets deliberate, low-stress loading twice a week from different angles. Thursday's lower work is kept submaximal on purpose: with the match two days later, you want fresh legs and an un-fatigued knee going into all that cutting and sprinting. Wednesday is light yoga at home, Saturday is your game, and Sunday is rest with optional gentle mobility.
Knee principles Let the left side set the standard on every single-leg movement and match the right to it — that's how rebalancing happens, and it won't if the strong side carries the load. Own the eccentric (the lowering) on a 2–3 second count; tendons and control respond to time under tension more safely than to speed. Progress depth and range gradually and never push through sharp pain — next-day swelling means it was too much, so scale back. Your Saturday match is the biggest test the knee faces all week, so treat the lifting as what makes it resilient, not as something to grind into the ground. Anything your physical therapist has told you overrides anything on this page.

Yoga here does two jobs at once — it opens the hips and hamstrings that get tight from sitting and lifting, and it trains the balance and proprioception your knee needs to feel trustworthy again. The whole approach is built to protect a healing joint rather than test it.

Knee-safe ground rules Pad the knee — a folded towel or blanket under it — in any kneeling pose; there's no toughness points for grinding bone into the mat. Avoid deep, loaded knee flexion early (full hero pose, deep lotus, an aggressive low lunge); use blocks and bolsters to shorten the range until depth feels genuinely easy. In every lunge and warrior, keep the front knee tracking over the ankle and never letting it drift inward. Favor the poses that build hip mobility, hamstring length, and single-leg balance — those are the ones that pay your knee back.

What to reach for, and why

Tree pose & single-leg balance — the most direct proprioception work you can do; the wobble is the point, and it's how the knee relearns to stabilize itself.

Bridge & locust — posterior-chain activation that strengthens around the joint without loading it through deep flexion.

Figure-four & supine pigeon — hip opening done lying down, so you get the release without putting the knee in a vulnerable position.

Legs-up-the-wall — genuinely useful for draining swelling and downshifting before sleep; hold it longer than feels necessary.

Eating as a vegetarian lifter The one thing worth getting right is protein spread across the day rather than loaded into one meal — a solid source at each (Greek yogurt, eggs, tofu, tempeh, lentils, beans, paneer, edamame, or a shake), since plant proteins are a touch less complete and your training plus the Saturday match raise what you need. Everything here is a flexible estimate built to fuel your training, not to restrict — and I'm not a dietitian, so treat guidance from a doctor or registered dietitian as the final word.
